Visual Direction A

Clean Minimal Light

Pure white and warm off-white backgrounds, generous whitespace, soft card shadows, calm neutral typography. Feels approachable and unintimidating for non-technical users. Progress is shown through simple cards and progress bars rather than complex data visualisations.

💡

Why this direction works for MOS Training

Tradespeople on site don't want complexity. A clean light interface reduces cognitive load, makes large touch targets obvious, and feels trustworthy — similar to what HMRC, NHS, and GOV.UK digital services use. Recommended direction.

Visual Direction B

Structured Utility App

Designed for people who do tasks for a living — field service, trades, and gig economy workers. Prominent CTAs, structured form layouts, strong colour accents (often green or blue) to signal actions. Feels purposeful and business-like rather than decorative.

Visual Direction C

Modern Dark / High-Contrast Utility

Pure black or deep navy backgrounds with white text and bright accent colours. Popular with utility/technical apps (Starlink, Apple Fitness, Opal). Excellent outdoor readability in direct sunlight — a real advantage on building sites. Feels premium and technical.

☀️

Outdoor readability advantage

Dark backgrounds with bright text can outperform light UIs in direct sunlight on modern OLED screens. If the target device is likely Android or iPhone with OLED, this direction has a practical benefit beyond aesthetics.

Visual Direction D

Enterprise Compliance Tool

Dense, information-forward layouts optimised for accuracy over aesthetics. Bold headlines, checkbox-heavy forms, warning banners, document upload areas, and explicit labelling (Required*, Mandatory). Used in regulated industries: fintech, HR/payroll, insurance. Signals seriousness and thoroughness.

📋

When to consider this direction

If the primary buyer is an Approved Centre or employer (rather than the apprentice) this direction can build trust with procurement teams. Risk: can feel bureaucratic for individual tradespeople using it daily on site.

The core of the app: a scrollable list of NVQ criteria grouped by learning outcome or unit. These references show proven patterns for accordion-grouped checklists with item counts, progress indicators, and collapsible sections.

Bold section headers with chevron toggles, circle checkboxes, add new placeholder. Minimal and familiar iOS pattern.
Target

"0 of 85 items completed", overall progress bar, then accordion sections each showing X/N complete. Perfect NVQ outcomes template.
Amazon Alexa
Categorised Checklist
Section headers in caps with item count (2), checkbox rows, completed items in a separate group. Crisp and readable.
Khan Academy

Category headers, selectable rows with checkboxes, "See All (N)" disclosure. Handles large lists gracefully.
Shopee
